Publishing Policies

Principles supporting ethical editorial decisions, fair peer review and a reliable scholarly record.

01

Publication Ethics

i-Proclaim is committed to responsible scholarly publishing and follows the core principles of the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE). Editors consider allegations fairly, confidentially and on the basis of available evidence.

Originality

Submissions must be original, properly referenced and not simultaneously submitted elsewhere. Text recycling, plagiarism and undisclosed duplicate publication are not acceptable.

Authorship

Authorship must be limited to individuals who made a substantial scholarly contribution and accept accountability for the work. Guest, gift and ghost authorship are prohibited.

Conflicts and funding

Authors, reviewers and editors must disclose relationships or interests that could reasonably influence their work. Authors must also identify relevant funding and the funder’s role.

Citations

References must be relevant and used for scholarly reasons. Coercive citation, citation trading and references added primarily to manipulate citation indicators are not permitted.

Confidentiality

Editors and reviewers must protect submitted manuscripts and related correspondence. Unpublished information may not be used for personal or professional advantage.

Concerns and misconduct

Suspected fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, unethical research or other misconduct may lead to review suspension, rejection, correction, retraction or referral to an appropriate institution.

Editorial action will be proportionate to the evidence and may follow relevant COPE guidance.

02

Research Integrity

Published research should be honest, transparent, reproducible where appropriate and respectful of research participants, communities and intellectual property.

Standards expected from authors

  • Report methods, data, analyses and limitations accurately without fabrication, falsification or misleading omission.
  • Obtain and disclose appropriate ethics approval, informed consent and permissions where required.
  • Retain underlying records and make data or supporting materials available when reasonably required, subject to ethical, legal and confidentiality limits.
  • Disclose the responsible use of generative AI or other automated tools when required by the journal. Such tools cannot be listed as authors.
  • Notify the journal promptly if a significant error or integrity concern is discovered before or after publication.

Submissions may be screened for similarity and other integrity concerns. A similarity score alone does not determine misconduct; editors review the context and evidence before taking action.

03

Peer Review

All research articles are first assessed by an editor for journal scope, basic quality, originality, ethical compliance and readiness for review. Manuscripts that pass initial screening are normally evaluated by independent specialists with relevant subject expertise.

  1. Editorial screening: The editor checks scope, presentation, declarations and potential integrity concerns.
  2. Reviewer selection: Reviewers are selected for expertise and independence and must disclose conflicts of interest.
  3. Evaluation: Reviewers consider originality, methodology, evidence, clarity, relevance and whether the conclusions are supported.
  4. Decision: The editor considers the reports and decides to accept, request minor or major revision, reject, or seek further review.
  5. Revision: Authors should respond to each reviewer comment clearly and identify all substantive changes.

Journals may use single-anonymous or double-anonymous review according to their stated editorial policy. Reviewers’ recommendations inform the decision, but final responsibility rests with the journal editor. Reviewers must maintain confidentiality and must not upload unpublished manuscripts to public or generative-AI systems.

The journal aims to conduct review efficiently, but no fixed decision or publication time is guaranteed because timing depends on reviewer availability and the extent of revision required.

04

Corrections and Retractions

i-Proclaim works to preserve an accurate and transparent scholarly record. Concerns about published content are assessed by the editor and, where appropriate, may involve the authors, reviewers, publisher or relevant institution.

Correction

A correction may be published when an error affects part of an article or its metadata but does not invalidate the principal findings. The notice will describe the change and link to the original article.

Retraction

An article may be retracted when findings are unreliable, publication is redundant, research is unethical, or serious plagiarism or manipulation is established. The article record and retraction notice will remain accessible.

Expression of concern

An expression of concern may be issued while a serious matter is unresolved or when an investigation is inconclusive or delayed.

Removal

Published content will be removed only in exceptional circumstances, such as a legal requirement, a serious privacy risk or content likely to cause substantial harm. A bibliographic notice may remain.

Minor changes that do not affect meaning, such as routine formatting corrections, may be made without a formal notice. Authors and readers may report concerns to support@i-proclaim.my.
